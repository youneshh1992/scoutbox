Explore the new features (Milestone 20 — Recruitment Analytics + Director Dashboard)

One sentence governs the whole milestone: measure the recruitment process, not the worth of the player or the scout. A director can now see how the club’s work moves — what is in flight, what has stopped, how long each step takes, whether endings were written down, and whether the demand the club itself wrote is being covered. There is no Recruitment Score, no Scout Score, no Player Success Score, no leaderboard and no per-person breakdown of anything: the server throws at boot if such a name reaches the registry, and asking the API for groupBy=scout is refused by name with an explanation.

  • 1 · Where it lives — Pro or Grassroots (as Maria Keane) → Recruitment → Director Dashboard. One more destination inside the Recruitment area that already exists. Still no seventh sidebar section — the same discipline M17, M18 and M19 kept.
  • 2 · Nothing new is stored — every figure is projected on read from Rooms, decisions, briefs, Nobody Missed reviews, Second Look items, watchlists and trials. There is no analytics table, no rollup and no cache, so a number on this page cannot disagree with the record it came from: it is that record, read differently. The suite proves it by comparing every source collection byte-for-byte across a full dashboard read.
  • 3 · One clock — every duration, funnel step and aging figure comes from history[].at, the timestamp M12’s audit helper has written since Milestone 12. No timestamp is invented and none is inferred from the order records happen to sit in.
  • 4 · The funnel is not a conversion funnel — M17 makes reopening first-class, so a room is counted once for every stage it ever reached, the payload carries monotonic: false, and reopenings are counted and shown. A club whose rooms cycle is not a club whose funnel is broken. A reopening the Room writes twice at the same instant is one transition, not two.
  • 4b · Compared with the period before — three period-activity counts (rooms opened, ended, decided) sit against the same length of time immediately before, because comparing seven days with thirty would make every figure look like a collapse. And when the previous period was zero there is no percentage: “up 3, from none last period”, never “+100%” and never Infinity. Current-state figures get no trend at all — the period does not apply to them.
  • 4c · Bucketed as well as summarised — open rooms appear both as a typical age and as 0–7 / 8–14 / 15–30 / 31–60 / over-60 buckets. Either alone misleads: a median of twenty days hides the four rooms sitting at two hundred. The page also says when it was calculated, because these are read-time projections and not a live feed.
  • 5 · Three answers, never collapsed into one — “Nothing in this period”, “0% of 12” and “1 of 4 — too few to express as a rate” are three different facts and render as three different sentences. A dashboard that shows all three as 0% is lying twice.
  • 6 · Withheld rather than misleading — any rate or median over fewer than five records is withheld and its raw counts shown instead. Counts themselves are never hidden: a director must be able to see their own three rooms. Try the demo’s Where the room came from → Matching filter to watch a rate refuse to be a rate.
  • 7 · Medians, never means — every duration shows a typical figure with its middle half and the number of records behind it. One eighteen-month room would move a mean and tell you nothing true about the other forty.
  • 8 · The exclusion is always on screen — a duration measures only what finished, which quietly flatters a club whose work is stuck: a stage where everything is stuck measures fast. So every duration names the records it could not include, and time-in-stage points explicitly at Rooms with no recent activity, the figure that catches what it cannot see.
  • 9 · Every panel prints its own limitation — not a tooltip, not an asterisk, not a help page. The caveat is a sentence in the metric registry, it travels with the number on the wire, and it renders directly under the figure. All 24 panels, counted by the live suite and by the demo spotcheck.
  • 10 · Association, never cause — one figure compares groups (stages reached, by source) and it is built defensively: the association sentence is in the payload so the number cannot render without it, sources are listed alphabetically so it can never read as a league table, and suppression below five is enforced without exception. Most sources in a real club will be withheld, and that is the correct outcome.
  • 11 · The one number you can actually act on — Endings limited by evidence. If a third of your endings are recorded as “we could not tell”, the constraint is the evidence reaching you, not the judgement being made — and unlike almost every analytics number, fixing it changes a process rather than a person.
  • 12 · Reopening and revising are marked neutral — a room that came back and a decision that was later revised are both shown, both uncoloured, and both captioned “this is not a fault”. Second Look exists to cause exactly these.
  • 13 · No person is a dimension — the filter row offers period, source, priority and a stall threshold: properties of the work. It says out loud, in English and in French, that there is no filter by colleague and there will not be one. Ask the API for one anyway and it refuses by name rather than pretending not to understand.
  • 14 · Leads only, and one organisation — the same requireLead every administrative surface uses; a scout sees their own Rooms, not the club-wide view. Every projection filters the organisation first. A player the club may not currently see is still counted but never named, because a count is not a disclosure.
  • 15 · A failing panel names itself — each of the seven families is computed inside its own boundary. One that cannot be worked out returns a named failure in its own slot with the reason (never a stack trace), and every other panel still renders. A dashboard that silently drops a panel is lying about coverage.
  • 16 · A shared link is the exact view — period and filters live in the address bar, so refresh, Back and a link pasted to a colleague reproduce what you are reading. The server re-validates every value, so an edited link widens nothing — and there is no filter in that link that could name a person.
Honest scope. M20 stores nothing and emits nothing. No analytics table, no rollup, no cache, no new live event, no new notification category — a dashboard that pushes numbers at people becomes a performance review by another route. Its migration creates only collections other milestones already own. A stalled room is not a neglected player: “no activity in ScoutBox” is not “no activity”, and every aging figure is a prompt to look rather than a finding. Duration metrics measure only what finished, and say so. Watchlist churn counts reconciliations, not moments a player’s facts changed, because M19 has no scheduler — a list nobody opens records nothing. Point-in-time figures cannot be asked about the past; “how many rooms were stalled last March” is a question this build declines rather than approximates. Small-n suppression genuinely hides information from small clubs, and that is the intended trade. The evaluation-coverage figures inherit M18’s definitions, right or wrong, so that there is one definition rather than two. Performance was measured at 100 / 500 / 1000 rooms (1.6 / 7.9 / 14.3 ms) and the result contradicted the design: the single-pass context the projection was built around is under a tenth of a read, and the families re-walking room history are the rest. That is documented rather than optimised, because a transition cache would be a second copy of a derivation this milestone promised not to keep. Five defects are written up in M20_MATRIX.md, including one with nothing to do with M20 — db.trials had only ever been created by the seed, so a snapshot restored without it would have crashed the trial-request safeguarding gate.

Explore the new features (Milestone 19 — Explainable Matching + Dynamic Watchlists)

Two questions, and one refusal. Explainable Matching answers which players we can already see satisfy the criteria our club wrote, and exactly why. Dynamic Watchlists answer keep that current as the facts change, and tell us what changed. Neither answers who is best. There is no match score, no talent or potential score, no ranking, no “recommended player” — and the words themselves are swept out of the source and the screens by the test suite, allowed only inside a sentence that denies them.

  • 1 · Where it lives — Pro (as Maria Keane) → Recruitment → Player Matching and Dynamic Watchlists. Two more destinations inside the Recruitment area that already exists. No seventh sidebar section, the same discipline M17 and M18 kept.
  • 2 · You write the criteria — eleven typed facts (position, age, area, level, foot, availability, evidence, evidence recency, evidence confidence, Combine result, Combine measurement) and eight operators, in two tables, in one file. Typed controls, never a free-text JSON box: a criteria set nobody can read back six weeks later is not a criteria set.
  • 3 · Required decides, preferred explains — a player must satisfy every required criterion to appear at all. Preferred criteria add context and are counted — “2 of 3 met” — never divided, normalised, or compared between players. Adding a preferred criterion changes the explanation and never the membership; the live suite asserts the list and its order are identical before and after.
  • 4 · Why this player matches — every card names every required criterion and whether it was met, in words: “Matches position criteria (CDM)”, “Evidence within the last 90 days”. Nothing on the screen matched for a reason ScoutBox will not print. The unit of the feature is the line, not the list.
  • 5 · A refusal is an answer — write an age range that starts after it ends and the editor keeps everything you typed and says “the range starts after it ends”, pointing at that row. An unknown criterion, operator or value is refused with its own code; nothing is ever silently dropped and evaluated as if it were fine.
  • 6 · Protected characteristics are not matchable — ethnicity, religion, disability, family income, school type and the rest are refused before any other validation runs, in whatever spelling they arrive, in either class. The editor never offers them; the wire refuses them anyway, because the interface is not the security boundary.
  • 7 · Authorization runs first, always — standing → authorization → visibility → blocks → minors and guardians → the grassroots radius → facts → then matching. Three organisations over the same data match over 14, 12 and 2 players. A player one club may see and another may not is simply absent from the second club’s answer, and there is no “considered” count that would reveal them.
  • 8 · Distance is an authorisation, not a column — the grassroots app shows distance because it is allowed to; the Pro app does not, and asking Pro to order by distance is refused, because an ordering must not leak what the field withholds. The geography explanation carries no number for anyone: it says “within the club’s permitted search area”. Explaining a match must never become a way to locate a child.
  • 9 · An ordering, not a ranking — five orderings, each naming what it orders by, every one ending on the player id so two reads give the same order, declared in the body and on the wire. None is called best, relevance or fit; ask for best_match and you get the declared default, because no such ordering exists. The preferred count is never a sort key.
  • 10 · Save it as a Dynamic Watchlist — a watchlist saves criteria, not a list of names. A stored list of players goes stale in exactly the ways that matter: someone is blocked, ages out, their evidence expires, and nobody wrote to the list to say so. Try 2027 Defensive Midfielders in the demo.
  • 11 · The mode is chosen, never guessed — Follow the Recruitment Brief (edit the brief, the list follows) or Keep these saved criteria (later brief edits do not touch it). Saving without choosing is refused, and editing the criteria of a brief-following list is refused with the two honest options named.
  • 12 · What changed, and why — “2 newly matched · 1 no longer matches”, with a history that names the criterion that flipped, or says plainly that the criteria changed. Nothing says a player got better or worse — that is asserted by the suite, not merely intended. Twenty-five players moving after a brief edit is one notification, in a category you can switch off.
  • 13 · Leaving is never explained away — a player who leaves because they blocked the club, or because a safeguarding rule changed, or because their account went, gets one reason: “no longer available in this watchlist”. Naming which one would leak exactly what the rule exists to protect.
  • 14 · Into a Room, and still on the list — Add to Recruitment Room calls the ordinary M17 Room creator, so every standing gate and the one-open-room rule behave as they do anywhere else; doing it twice opens the same Room, not a second one. The player stays on the watchlist while they still match: being evaluated is not a reason to stop tracking whether they meet your criteria.
  • 15 · A shared link, a shared conflict — a search you ran lives in the address bar, so refresh, Back and a link to a colleague all reproduce it (the server re-validates every criterion, so an edited link widens nothing). And if a colleague renames the watchlist while you are renaming it, you get the same conflict notice every other surface uses, with their name on it and every word you typed still there.
Honest scope. There is no scheduler in this build. Nothing recomputes while the application is closed: membership is derived when a watchlist is read, it is correct as of that read, and the page says when that was. That is why it is a Dynamic Watchlist and not a live one, and it is stated in the payload, on the screen and in the documentation rather than papered over. The candidate scan is linear in the number of players and capped — there is no index over positions or ages, because the snapshot store has no query planner to give one to; when the cap truncates a scan the response says so instead of presenting a partial answer as complete. Preferred criteria are counted and will never be scored. Discover’s own ordering was not changed: M19 adds a separate surface where the club owns the ordering question outright. Nothing here is player-facing — no player is told that a club is matching on them, which criteria they met, or that they entered or left a list — and Trust & Safety has no routine access to any of it. Six defects were found by the suites during this milestone and fixed rather than documented around; two of them, a foot criterion that could never match a real player and a live refetch that would have silently overwritten a colleague’s rename, are written up in M19_MATRIX.md.

What changed in Milestone 18.2 — Final Pre-M19 Cleanup

No new recruitment concept, and deliberately no M19: no Explainable Matching, no Dynamic Watchlists, no player score. M18.2 removes ambiguity — a notification nobody could mute, a conflict told two different ways, an ordering nobody had declared, a schema nobody had versioned, an age computed in whatever time zone the server happened to be in — so that M19 is built on decisions that are written down.

  • 1 · Choose what reaches you — Organisation → Notification preferences. Twelve categories; mentions, assignments, Room changes, evidence, Combine, trials, Second Look, Briefs, messages and outcomes are on; discovery nudges are off; Security and account is always on and says why. A muted category produces nothing — not a hidden row, nothing. Email is described as what it is in this build: a local outbox, never delivery.
  • 2 · One conflict, one experience — when a colleague changes the Room or Brief you are editing, the same notice appears on both, names who changed it, keeps every word you typed, and offers Reload latest or Keep my changes. The live suite found the first version showed the stale Brief after Reload; it re-reads now.
  • 3 · You will not lose a form by accident — leaving a Brief with unsaved changes asks first, whether you use the sidebar, the address bar, Back, reload or close. A clean form never asks. The first implementation could let a dirty form go on a hash change; the live suite caught it and the decision now happens before anything changes.
  • 4 · Discover says how it is ordered — “Ordered by Academy+ opt-in, then profile completeness, then player id … not ability, and not the Trust Score.” The order itself was deliberately kept; it is now deterministic (two reads, same order), declared on the wire, and stated on the screen. M18_2_SORTING_AUDIT.md lists every ordered surface and says which could be misread as a ranking.
  • 5 · The organisation’s own record — Organisation → Organisation activity: what happened and who did it, across Rooms, Briefs and staff changes, for leads only. It says that a note was written and never shows the note, a comment or a decision’s text. A scout sees a permission message — and stays signed in; a 403 is about one request, not the session.
  • 6 · Destructive actions say what they do — archive, close, delete, cancel, remove and record signing each state their consequence (“Decision history, comments and tasks are preserved”) and never ask “are you sure?”. Pausing a Brief or dismissing a Second Look item needs no confirmation, because undoing them is one click away.
  • 7 · Every event is registered — the server’s 18 events sit in one registry with an audience, a privacy class and a payload allowlist; a key that is not allowlisted does not ship, and development refuses to boot if a broadcast is not in the registry. A Room archival now carries two ids and nothing else.
  • 8 · Provenance has one vocabulary — the Passport, the Room and the Second Look changes all read from one table. An unknown source says “Source unavailable” and credits nobody; it is never guessed as “Player-provided”.
  • 9 · Dates that mean the same everywhere — a person is 18 from the first UTC instant of their birthday in every time zone the server runs in; a leap-day birth is 18 on 1 March; a Korean 18-year-old is not an adult and a British one is. A Brief window is inclusive calendar days at both ends. Second Look’s 30-day cooldown and 120-day expiry are tested at the exact millisecond.
  • 10 · Honest to whoever runs it — a schema version on /healthz, on /capabilities and on every response; versioned, idempotent, recorded migrations that upgrade a pre-M18.2 snapshot in place; a boot-time integrity report (one open Room per club and player, unique ids, valid revisions) that reports and never repairs; every error says whether retrying could help, and a 429 says when. Slow and failing sources can be simulated in development and are refused outright in production.
Honest scope. Discover’s ordering was not replaced — it is declared and made deterministic; changing it is an M19 decision. Email is an intent recorded for when a transport exists. Push quiet hours are still evaluated in server-local time, because they gate a push transport this build does not have. A player-id index was measured and deliberately not added (a scan costs under a microsecond at this scale; the threshold is written down). The notification bell collapses identical repeats but does not yet fold different updates about one object into one row. No Explainable Matching, no Dynamic Watchlists, no AI recommendation, no player ranking or match score, no new sidebar item — M19 starts from here, not inside here.

What changed in Milestone 18.1 — Production Hardening & UX Cleanup

No new recruitment concept. M18.1 is the milestone that goes back over what already shipped and fixes what was quietly wrong: a lost update, a number with two meanings, a badge that mislabelled where evidence came from, a failure with no way out of it, and a set of claims about this deployment that nobody outside the source code could check.

  • 1 · Two people, one record — two scouts editing the same Recruitment Brief, or moving the same Room, used to silently overwrite each other. Every collaborative record now carries a revision; a write built on a stale read is refused, and the refusal says nothing of theirs was overwritten and offers Reload. Of two simultaneous decisions exactly one lands.
  • 2 · A conflict you cannot miss — the first version showed it as a toast, and a routine notification replaced it within a second. It now stays on the Room panel until you act on it.
  • 3 · Two numbers, two names — the older profile figure and the M16.2 Trust Score were both shown as “Trust”. The first is now Profile completeness everywhere, in three apps and both languages. Trust Score means evidence confidence and nothing else.
  • 4 · A missing value is never a score — a Room whose evidence confidence could not be read showed “—”, one glance from reading as zero confidence. It now says which of the two things happened. In the player app every Combine state has its own words: an attempt invalidated after review no longer looks identical to one your phone simply could not measure, and its value is struck through and labelled as no longer counting.
  • 5 · Provenance that never mislabels — evidence ScoutBox itself observed through Box Cam was badged “Player-provided” in the club apps and rendered as a raw identifier in the player app. It has its own label now, deliberately in the neutral colour: Box Cam observation ranks below a ScoutBox review and must not borrow a confirmation badge.
  • 6 · Failures have a way out — a dropped connection in Second Look, Nobody Missed, Briefs or Rooms used to render a message and stop. Every one now offers Try again and announces itself to a screen reader. The four Second Look tabs each say what their emptiness means instead of sharing one line.
  • 7 · A Passport revision that is a fact, not a clock — a decision snapshot can now name exactly which Passport truth it saw. The revision is a hash of the records themselves, so the same Passport read a year later produces the same token; if it included “days since last evidence” it would change overnight on a read and mean nothing.
  • 8 · Notifications worth having — an identical unread notification no longer arrives twice or pushes your phone twice, and rows that lead somewhere are now buttons that take you there. A week-old “14:32” carries its date.
  • 9 · Nothing leaks by default — event delivery used to infer its audience from the shape of the payload and fall through to “everyone”. Every event is classified, and an unclassified one reaches nobody. The adult date of birth, which no club surface displayed, is gone from the projection entirely.
  • 10 · It works on a phone — the top bar wraps, the notification panel fits the screen, wide tables scroll themselves instead of dragging the page, and four recruitment surfaces measure zero horizontal overflow at 390px in a real browser.
  • 11 · Honest to whoever runs it — /capabilities reports what this deployment can actually do: no production computer vision, no distributed rate limiting, which providers are configured. It contains no keys or hostnames, and it is deliberately hard to inflate. Asking for a shared rate-limit backend that does not exist is refused loudly rather than falling back to per-process counting behind your back.
Honest scope. Rate limiting is now one policy table behind one provider interface, but there is still no shared backend: limits are counted per process, which is wrong behind a load balancer, and the capability report says exactly that instead of implying protection that does not exist. Discover still orders by profile completeness — that ordering is now stated rather than silently applied, and changing it is a product decision, not a hardening fix. There is no notification preference system yet. And Trust & Safety still has no access to private recruitment decisions: the shape a scoped, reasoned, time-bound, audited, read-only grant would take is written down, and deliberately not built, because a blanket support role over a club’s decisions is worse than none.

Explore the new features (Milestone 18 — Second Look + Nobody Missed)

Clubs lose players two ways: they archive someone for a reason that later stops being true and never find out, and they never look at someone who matched what they said they wanted. Second Look asks has something materially changed since we decided? Nobody Missed asks who matches our own written brief and never entered our workflow? Neither decides who is talented, who deserves a contract, or who was wrongly rejected.

  • 1 · The queue — Pro (as Maria Keane) → Recruitment → Second Look. Four tabs on one page — Worth Another Look · Evidence Changed · Reviewed · Dismissed — inside the Recruitment area that already exists.
  • 2 · Reason-aware, not score-aware — Diego Alonso was archived for not enough recent evidence. A full match and a verified coach reference have since landed, and the card says so and names the reason recorded at the time. It never says the decision was wrong — a copy sweep in the suite enforces that.
  • 3 · The honest half — the same card prints Reasons that still stand: Squad space. Squad space, budget, timing, registration, travel and eligibility are facts about the club. No amount of new footage resolves them, and nothing here pretends otherwise.
  • 4 · One change, one item — a single Combine result reaches M18 through the Combine projection, the Passport and the Trust components. The fingerprint is type:source:id without the timestamp, so that is one change, not three alerts — and a club that archived the same player twice still gets one item, against its most recent decision.
  • 5 · Review changes — At your previous review vs now, built from the snapshot captured when the decision was made. Only rows that moved are shown, and a field the snapshot never recorded reads “Previous detail unavailable” — never a fabricated value, never a misleading zero.
  • 6 · Reopen is a click — press Reopen room, pick a reason, and the reopen goes through the Room’s own rules: same transition table, same snapshot capture, same activity trail, and the archived decision stays in history. ScoutBox never reopens a room for you.
  • 7 · Dismissal is private and final for that evidence — dismissing is organisation-private, changes nothing about the player, and the same evidence can never regenerate the item however many times the queue is re-read. Genuinely new evidence can, immediately.
  • 8 · Recruitment Briefs — Pro → Recruitment Briefs → 2027 Defensive Midfielder: positions, ages, radius, level, evidence requirements, an optional evidence-confidence band. Every criterion is one the club typed. There are no hidden criteria, no weights and no ranking.
  • 9 · Protected characteristics are refused, not dropped — race, ethnicity, religion, disability, family income, school type, postcode deprivation, physical maturity and national origin are each rejected by their own error code, so the refusal is unambiguous in an audit.
  • 10 · Nobody Missed — Evaluation Coverage over that brief: 8 eligible · 5 evaluated · 3 not yet · 63%. It is workflow coverage — never Scout Quality, Recruitment Quality, a Scouting Score or a Fairness Score — and the panel says so itself.
  • 11 · “Why is this player here?” — every candidate lists the club’s own criteria, met and not met. Boolean rules only: unknown age and unknown distance fail closed, exactly like the standing gates, and visibility, blocks, minors and the grassroots radius run before matching, never after.
  • 12 · Looking is not evaluating — nine signals count as a real evaluation (a room, a case, a submitted assessment, a trial, a signing, a shortlist, a deferral, a squad entry); a profile view, a search impression and a Passport view explicitly do not. Add a candidate to a Room and coverage really moves to 6 of 8.
  • 13 · The two queues never claim the same player — archive that room and they do not fall back into Nobody Missed: they were evaluated. Later relevant evidence reaches you through Second Look instead. And the player app contains neither system — no Second Look, no brief, no coverage, no archive reason, ever.
Honest scope. The two change types M18 declared but could not emit — position changed and current club confirmed — are emitted as of Milestone 18.1, each from a clock that genuinely dates the change rather than one borrowed from a different event; records written before M18.1 have no such clock and still emit nothing. Player unavailable can never be resolved: ScoutBox emits no availability-changed event, and inventing one would be worse than the gap. Analytics are organisation-private counters with no scout ranking of any kind — M18 measures workflow coverage, not employee performance — and Trust & Safety has no M18 surface at all.

Explore the new features (Milestone 17 — Recruitment Rooms)

Football Passport = the player’s truth layer. Recruitment Room = the club’s decision layer. A Room is the private internal workspace where recruitment staff evaluate one player together: what we reviewed, what we’re waiting on, who is doing it next, and what we decided — and why. The player owns the Passport; the club owns its Room, and never the other way round.

  • 1 · Open a Room — Pro (as Maria Keane) → Discover → Kola Adeyemi → Add to Recruitment Room. The Room opens on its own deep link inside Recruitment — not a new sidebar item, because Recruitment already exists.
  • 2 · Everything authorised, in one place — nine tabs: Overview, Passport, Evidence, Assessments, Combine, Development, Discussion, Activity, Decision. The Room composes the canonical Passport, Trust Score, Combine and Box Cam projections; it re-derives nothing and stores no copy of the player’s record.
  • 3 · Adding a player widens nothing — every standing rule re-runs on every read: the agency/minor wall, verified-club requirement, grassroots 50 km radius, blocks and suspension. A Room can never be used to bookmark a player the club is forbidden to see.
  • 4 · Trust Score, still evidence confidence — shown with “Evidence confidence — not football ability” every single time. The Room deliberately hides the older safeguarding score so there is never a second number called “trust” in the same header.
  • 5 · Decision readiness is counts, not a score — “Assessments 2/2 · Recent full match: available · Coach reference: none · Combine: no verified result · Open tasks: 1”, with room health as a word (Ready for review · Waiting on evidence · Trial pending). There is no Decision Score and no Signing Probability anywhere in M17.
  • 6 · Private discussion, tasks and assignments — threaded comments with @mentions of colleagues, tombstoned deletion that keeps the author attributable, and staff tasks that are never sent to the player. A player-facing ask must go through the evidence-request or Combine workflow instead.
  • 7 · Asking the player for something is still the player’s rules — Request evidence goes through the standing engine: deterministic rules, a 14-day anti-pestering window, guardian routing for under-18s and whitelisted wording. A scout has no free-text field to a player from inside a private room.
  • 8 · Decision memory — the Decision tab records a recommendation with a structured reason from a fixed taxonomy (football · evidence · process · outcome). Decisions are append-only: a revision supersedes its predecessor and never rewrites it.
  • 9 · No protected characteristic, ever — race, nationality, religion, disability, socioeconomic background and the rest are refused by their own error code, not silently dropped. Internal tags are checked the same way. A club may decline a player; it may not record a protected trait as the reason.
  • 10 · What the club saw when it decided — shortlisting, a trial decision, an offer, a signing or an archive captures an immutable snapshot. Later the Room shows “Trust at decision: 78” beside “Current: 86” — and the first number never changes.
  • 11 · Archive, and the foundation for Second Look — archiving requires a reason; try it without one and it is refused. The archive reason is machine-readable, so Second Look (Milestone 18, above) can ask “has the reason this player was archived stopped applying?” without reading anyone’s private notes. Reopening is a first-class move and both events stay on the timeline.
  • 12 · Two clubs, two sealed rooms — Eastport and Harbour City can both evaluate the same player and neither knows the other’s room exists. A deep link to another club’s room returns exactly what a room that never existed returns. And the player app has no Recruitment Room at all — no status, no discussion, no reasoning, ever.
Honest scope. Trials and contact requests are linked from a Room rather than raised inside it: asking for a trial is a contact with a player or guardian and must pass its own consent path — a private room must not shortcut it. There is no AI recruiter, no signing probability and no player score; assessments remain evaluator opinion, Combine results remain measurements, the Trust Score remains evidence confidence, and ScoutBox never fuses them into a rating. Trust & Safety has no routine read access to any club’s room.

Explore the new features (Milestone 16.2 — ScoutBox Trust Score)

ScoutBox Trust Score reflects verification and evidence confidence — not football ability or recruitment suitability. It answers exactly one question: how strongly is this player’s football record supported by trustworthy, current and attributable evidence? It is derived at read time from what already exists — it stores nothing, decides nothing, and unlocks nothing.

  • 1 · Your Trust Profile — Player (as Kola Adeyemi) → You → ScoutBox Trust Score: a 0–100 score with a neutral evidence band (Limited · Developing · Established · Strong · Very strong) and the disclaimer travelling with it wherever it is shown.
  • 2 · “Why this score?” — expand it and every one of the six components is explained: Identity 15 · Football history 20 · Relationships 20 · Evidence 20 · Combine 15 · References 10, each with its coverage, what strengthened it (✓) and what could strengthen it further (○).
  • 3 · A low score is limited evidence, never bad character — the words untrustworthy, suspicious, risky and poor player exist nowhere in the product. A gap reads “1 club entry is provided by the player and not independently confirmed”, never an accusation.
  • 4 · Performance can never buy trust — this is the invariant the whole engine is built around. 80 touches and 220 touches with the same integrity contribute exactly the same. Not one measured value is an input; only whether a standardised protocol was genuinely measured.
  • 5 · Grinding does not work — 500 Box Cam sessions and 100 Combine attempts with no identity, no confirmed history and no references stays under 40, proven by an adversarial test. Component caps, per-source sub-caps and diminishing-return curves make volume worthless.
  • 6 · Coverage, not repetition — Combine counts distinct protocols, so repeating one test ten times is one test’s worth; a partially-measured attempt earns no Combine Verified contribution at all.
  • 7 · Fact-specific authority — there is no universal “who is most trustworthy” rank. A club is strong authority for its own affiliation; a coach for the coach–player relationship; Box Cam for the activity it observed and not for club history, from which it is absent entirely.
  • 8 · Prestige, popularity and payment are not inputs — a famous club and a park-side club give identical relationship confidence; the engine never sees a club name at all. Followers, trials, signings and money change nothing. A verified grassroots record can reach a strong band with no professional affiliation whatsoever.
  • 9 · Fair to minors and to newcomers — a category that cannot exist for a minor (agency representation) leaves the denominator rather than scoring zero, and a new player with an empty record is never buried: the score is informational, never a discovery gate and never a sort order.
  • 10 · What a club sees — Pro (as Maria Keane) → open a player → Trust Profile: the score, the band, component levels and safe evidence signals, with the tooltip “Evidence confidence — not football ability.” The player’s own gaps, source records and guardian details never cross over.
  • 11 · Removal is not punishment — an invalidated Combine result or Box Cam session stops contributing immediately, and the score becomes exactly that of a player who never had it. A legitimate restoration returns the same contribution. Nobody is blamed for a provider bug.
  • 12 · A score is not authorization, and cannot be typed — a Trust Score of 100 grants zero additional permissions: every safeguarding rule, block, radius and suspension refuses exactly as before. T&S → Cases → Trust inspects the full derivation but has no write route — a wrong score is fixed by correcting the evidence or the policy, then recalculating.
Honest scope. Because no production Combine capability exists here (see Milestone 16.1), Combine Confidence is honestly 0 in a real production profile, and the demo runs on the clearly-labelled simulated provider — simulated evidence never contributes to a production score. Identity assurance is likewise capped by reality: with no authoritative identity provider configured, the strongest real identity today is a ScoutBox document review, deliberately scored below authoritative. The Trust Score is not a talent score, not a recommendation and not a permission.

Explore the new features (Milestone 16.1 — At-Home Combine, powered by Box Cam)

Standardised drills. Real measurable numbers. Recorded live with Box Cam. — Record it. Prove it. Put a number on it. At-Home Combine turns Box Cam into standardised tests and produces a number a club can trust — Combine Verified — only when ScoutBox can genuinely measure it.

  • 1 · My Combine — Player (as Kola Adeyemi) → You → At-Home Combine: verified results, personal bests, the standardised drill library, and any Club Combine a club has requested.
  • 2 · A standardised protocol — open Box Touch 60: one fixed protocol (60-second window, defined setup, camera, space, scoring), pinned to a version so a historical result is never rewritten by a later change.
  • 3 · Ready Check & calibration — Start My Combine runs the Box Cam Ready Check and standardised geometry calibration, then a live capture — the same integrity flow as Box Cam.
  • 4 · The verified number, server-derived — the counter runs, you finish, and the result — e.g. 184 touches — is calculated by ScoutBox from what it observed. You cannot type, edit or upload a number. Your work counts.
  • 5 · Combine Verified, explained — a verified result carries a “Why is this verified?” checklist: standardised protocol ✓, live Box Cam capture ✓, liveness ✓, calibration ✓, server-calculated ✓, detector supported the metric ✓, integrity ✓ — far more than a blue tick.
  • 6 · Combine Card — a collection of your standardised measurements captured with Box Cam. It is not an overall rating — no athletic score, no averaging; clubs decide what matters.
  • 7 · Honest capability — most tests show “Measurement not yet supported on this device” and are never estimated. In this demo they run on a clearly-labelled simulated observer; athletic tests (sprints, jumps) need a detector no environment here has, so they are architected but never faked.
  • 8 · Partial, never projected — if the standardised window is not fully observed, the attempt is measured, not Combine Verified, and records exactly what was seen — no extrapolation to a full-window figure.
  • 9 · Football Passport — verified Combine results project onto the Passport (best per protocol version, “Captured by Box Cam”), shared with a club only on the player’s/guardian’s recruitment opt-in.
  • 10 · Club Combine — Pro (as Maria Keane) → open a player → At-Home Combine: request standardised tests (the club selects, it can never change a protocol’s rules) with a deadline. The club sees results only, never home footage.
  • 11 · Compare, without ranking — the club comparison shows verified numbers with blank cells for missing results (never 0) and produces no overall ranking.
  • 12 · Completion ≠ selection — completing a Club Combine means the standardised tests were done and Combine Verified — never that the club has scouted, selected or endorsed the player. Trust & Safety can invalidate a result through its bound Box Cam session (never type a replacement number).
Honest scope. No production computer-vision model, automated timing gate or physical-scale detector exists here, and web capture observes presence + active duration only — so no first-library Combine is production-verified today; all are exercised through the clearly-labelled simulated provider. At-Home Combine records standardised measurements, not a talent score, and implies no ScoutBox endorsement.

Explore the new features (Milestone 16 — Box Cam + Development Intelligence)

Box Cam is ScoutBox’s first-party observed-training evidence system. Train. Record. Prove. — don’t just say you trained, box it. It records what ScoutBox could reliably observe during a live drill, and nothing more.

  • 1 · Start Box Cam — Player (as Kola Adeyemi) → You → Box Training → “Train in the Box”. A Box Cam Ready Check checks what it can measure (camera, live stream, orientation) and honestly says “unable to check automatically” for lighting, framing and space. Then you record a supported drill, live.
  • 2 · Observation ≠ evaluation — the result states only “Box Cam observed activity consistent with this supported drill for X active minutes/repetitions during a live capture session.” Never “performed perfectly”, never a talent score, never an ability judgement.
  • 3 · Active time is not session time — the result shows verified active time separately from total session duration. Standing still, leaving frame and water breaks do not count as active — elapsed time never substitutes for observed work.
  • 4 · Partial credit, never “Failed” — a target reached part-way (say 13:41 of 20:00) records as “Target not yet completed” and keeps the work. Completion comes strictly from verified values — 149 of 150 is not “completed”, and nothing is rounded up.
  • 5 · Honest about capability — Box Wall ships rep-counting as not configured: it records active duration and says so, rather than inventing a rep count. There is no production computer-vision model in these demos — it is stated as not configured, and the demo uses a clearly-labelled simulated observer, never presented as real.
  • 6 · Box Cam Verified provenance — a completed live session earns “Captured by Box Cam”, ranked above player-submitted but deliberately below coach- and club-confirmed. An uploaded video can never earn it — only the live capture flow can.
  • 7 · Coach-assigned Box Training — Pro (as Maria Keane) → open Kola Adeyemi → Box Training: assign a supported drill with a target. Coaches receive results only, never raw home footage, and every state stays non-punitive.
  • 8 · Streaks that respect rest — Box Streak counts consecutive training weeks with at least one verified session — rest days never break it. Box Best compares only within the same drill version. There is no global leaderboard.
  • 9 · Box Challenge, no endorsement — ScoutBox and verified-club challenges each carry the disclaimer that completing one does not mean a club has scouted, selected or endorsed you. Club challenges never bypass the standing visibility rules.
  • 10 · Development Activity on the Passport — meaningful sessions surface on the Football Passport as an aggregate summary that repeats “evidence of recorded training activity … not proof of player ability.” The career timeline is not flooded; detail stays in Box Training.
  • 11 · Trust & Safety back office — T&S → Cases → Box Cam: invalidate a result (or let it stand) with a written, audited reason, and restore an invalidated one — but T&S can never fabricate verified duration, repetition counts or completion.
  • 12 · Privacy by construction — no raw session video is retained by default, there is no audio, no scene recognition and no facial recognition; liveness proves live-session presence, not identity. For under-18s the guardian controls sharing, clips, challenges and assignment acceptance, and clubs get results, never footage.
Honest scope. Box Cam’s web capture verifies player presence and active duration only; production computer vision, native iOS/Android capture and real biometric identity are not implemented. Box Cam proves observed training activity — not ability — and implies no ScoutBox endorsement.

Explore the new features (Milestone 15 — Football Passport)

  • 1 · One football history — Player (as Kola Adeyemi) → You → Football Passport: current status, club history, timeline, evidence, references and achievements in one place — and every important fact shows where it came from.
  • 2 · Honest provenance everywhere — chips like “Provided by player”, “Coach confirmed ✓”, “Club confirmed ✓”; tap an item’s “Where is this from?” line for the plain-language copy (e.g. “ScoutBox has not independently confirmed this item”). Never a bare “Verified”.
  • 3 · A year is enough — add club history with just a year. The Passport keeps the precision you give it (2018 stays “2018”) and never invents dates.
  • 4 · The conflict engine — the demo passport shows a confirmed current club and a differing self entry: the confirmed record displays, the self view explains the conflict, and a correction path is offered. Nothing is silently overwritten — and nobody is accused.
  • 5 · Coverage, not scores — evidence coverage is a word (strong/moderate/limited), gaps are non-shaming suggestions, and the search-eligibility preview counts only generic checks — never a club’s private criteria. There is no player rating anywhere.
  • 6 · Revocable sharing — create a public link (safe public view: age, never date of birth) or a club link that only opens inside a verified club session and re-runs every safeguarding rule. For under-18s, sharing is guardian-controlled — the child’s app says so.
  • 7 · Recruitment view — Pro (as Maria Keane) → Players: search cards carry passport chips from one batch call; open a player and expand Football Passport for the club-side projection — your organisation’s assessments and trials only, never another club’s.
  • 8 · Club confirmations — a verified club can confirm a player’s achievement, upgrading its provenance to “Club confirmed ✓” without rewriting the player’s entry. Unverified organisations can’t.
  • 9 · History is never rewritten — coach references keep their submission-time snapshot: after a coach departs, the copy reads “Coach affiliation was verified when this reference was submitted”.
  • 10 · Trust & Safety back office — T&S → Cases → Passport: the correction queue (every resolution needs a written reason), the share registry with an instant kill-switch, and the source-graph inspector tracing every passport item to its source record.

How the demos behave

  • Everything runs on synthetic sample data — no real players, clubs, credentials or media. Each app shows an “Interactive demo — sample data” badge with its build identifier.
  • Demo state lives inside the page you have open and resets when you reload. Nothing you type is stored anywhere or reaches a real person.
  • The three standalone apps do not share accounts or messages with each other — each simulates its counterparties. To see the apps genuinely talking to one another, use the Connected Demo, where all three run in one page over one shared simulation.
  • Safeguarding flows are live in the demos: contacting an under-18 routes to their guardian, and the child’s view never contains the adult conversation. Box Cam runs on a clearly-labelled simulated observer — no real camera analysis takes place. Player Matching and Dynamic Watchlists run entirely in the page against the synthetic dataset, and produce no score or ranking of any kind. The Director Dashboard runs the same way, over nine synthetic rooms — small enough that several of its rates are honestly withheld rather than computed, which is the demonstration rather than a shortcoming of it. The Trust Scores shown are fixtures computed by the real policy arithmetic over synthetic evidence, and are labelled as simulated.
Live cross-device usage requires a hosted backend. These artifacts are self-contained demos. Real accounts, real-time messaging between different people’s devices, and persistent data need the ScoutBox server (Milestone 11) deployed to a host — it is built and tested in the repository, but not deployed as part of this demo.
